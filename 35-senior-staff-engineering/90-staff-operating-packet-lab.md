# Lab: Build a staff operating packet

You are the technical lead for reducing AI integration risk across six product teams. There is no direct reporting authority. Teams use three providers, two orchestration libraries, inconsistent evaluation, and broad service credentials. Leadership wants faster delivery, security wants centralized controls, and product teams fear another slow platform mandate.

Your packet must let the organization decide and execute without depending on private conversations.

## Part one: scope and ambiguity

Write:

- the organizational outcome, baseline, constraints, invariants, non-goals, and scope boundary;
- a map of facts, forecasts, assumptions, preferences, and policy questions;
- the five highest-consequence unknowns, experiments, owners, and decision dates;
- your portfolio tradeoff, including work you will stop.

## Part two: strategy and vision

Produce a diagnosis, three guiding policies, coordinated actions, exclusions, and review triggers. Add a technical vision with current constraints, future capabilities, architecture principles, extension points, transition states, and deprecation. Distinguish commitments from hypotheses.

## Part three: decision and influence

Write an RFC for centralizing identity, audit, quotas, and evaluation contracts while preserving product-owned prompts and task measures. Include alternatives, evidence, consequences, migration, operations, cost, security, dissent, decision owner, and revisit triggers.

Map people who decide, advise, execute, bear burden, and are absent. Prepare one-page briefs for an application operator, product leader, security owner, and executive. Facts must remain consistent while relevance changes. Record explicit commitments rather than “alignment.”

## Part four: execution and risk

Create end-to-end milestones, interface contracts, dependency ledger, decision-rights map, adoption plan, migration support, risk register, and stop conditions. Every risk includes cause, event, consequence, detection, mitigation, owner, residual exposure, and escalation trigger. Write one concise escalation that asks for a real decision.

## Part five: capability and stewardship

Delegate RFC facilitation and incident leadership to two engineers. Specify context, authority, support, checkpoints, and evidence of independent capability. Define platform maintainers, support, contribution, objectives, review cadence, succession, deprecation, and deletion of obsolete controls.

## Part six: ethics and AI judgment

Write an impact assessment covering affected groups, privacy, access, uneven quality, labor, appeal, and incident disclosure. Define which controls belong in the platform, which remain with products, and why. Include build-buy-exit analysis, artifact lineage, evaluation slices, human authority, cost per useful outcome, and a path for justified exceptions.

## Quality bar

The packet is strong when each proposed mechanism traces to an outcome or risk, burden and decision rights are explicit, uncertainty is honestly classified, dissent is preserved, execution is evidenced end to end, and ownership survives your absence. It is weak when title substitutes for authority, all concerns become platform scope, activity substitutes for outcome, or “best practice” substitutes for a mechanism.

## Completion

Take the packet into the [organizational pressure simulation](91-organizational-pressure-simulation-lab.md). Give reviewers the materials before you explain them; their independent understanding is part of the evidence.
