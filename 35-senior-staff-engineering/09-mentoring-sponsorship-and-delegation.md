# Mentoring, sponsorship, and delegation

Technical leadership grows capability by transferring context, opportunity, authority, and feedback rather than retaining the most consequential work.

## Why it matters

Heroic experts limit organizational capacity, and informal opportunity allocation can reproduce bias while appearing meritocratic.

## How it works

Mentoring helps another person reason and choose; coaching uses questions and feedback; teaching supplies a model or skill; sponsorship spends credibility to create visible opportunity. Name which relationship is offered, maintain consent and boundaries, and do not confuse performance management with confidential mentoring.

Delegate an outcome with context, constraints, decision rights, resources, checkpoints, and escalation conditions. Match the stretch to support. Let the owner make reversible choices and receive credit. Intervene for safety, hidden context, or agreed escalation, not because their path differs from yours.

Allocate sponsorship by explicit capability goals and fair access, not familiarity. Give specific behavioral feedback close to the event and invite correction. Measure transfer by independent detection, decisions, delivery, and recovery. Succession is complete when others can carry authority under realistic pressure.

## See it yourself

Handing someone an RFC to type is task delegation. Giving them the decision, stakeholder access, constraints, review support, and authority to recommend is ownership development. If you privately rewrite the recommendation before review, ownership never transferred.

## Where it shows up

Design reviews, incident command, roadmap ownership, migrations, and standards provide consequential learning when authority and support are genuine.

## When it breaks

Mentoring breaks through cloning, rescue, vague feedback, sponsorship for favorites, and delegation without access. Ask whether the learner owns a decision, can fail safely, and receives visible credit.

## Practice

**Build:** create a capability plan that transfers design-review and incident-lead authority to two engineers. Include opportunity criteria, support, decision rights, feedback, and evidence of independence. **Break:** one engineer chooses a sound approach you dislike. **Explain back:** decide whether intervention serves safety or your preference.

## Check yourself

1. How does sponsorship differ from mentoring?
2. What authority must accompany delegated ownership?
3. Which evidence demonstrates capability transfer?
