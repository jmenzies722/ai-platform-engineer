# Risk and responsible escalation

Technical leadership makes risk concrete enough for the right person to reduce, transfer, accept, or avoid it.

## Why it matters

Unowned risk becomes surprise, while dramatic but unquantified warnings erode credibility and can conceal who has authority to choose.

## How it works

Write risk as cause, uncertain event, and consequence. Assess likelihood, impact, detectability, time horizon, and affected groups; numbers are ranges, not decoration. Record prevention, detection, mitigation, contingency, owner, review trigger, and residual exposure.

Distinguish technical uncertainty from value conflict and policy violation. Engineering recommends and explains consequence; the authorized business, security, privacy, or safety owner accepts residual risk. Escalate when harm crosses authority, the owner is absent, evidence is suppressed, a legal or ethical duty applies, or delay closes safe options.

Escalation should be early, factual, bounded, and accompanied by options. State decision needed, deadline, evidence, consequence of no action, and your recommendation. Preserve the record. Continue respectful disagreement, but do not let “disagree and commit” erase duties to users or the public.

## See it yourself

A launch lacks tenant-isolation testing. “Security is worried” is weak. “A shared retrieval path has no negative authorization test; a defect could expose customer documents; validation takes two days; launch owner must delay, reduce scope, or formally accept this exposure by Thursday” is actionable.

## Where it shows up

Risk leadership covers migrations, capacity, data deletion, vendor dependency, AI harms, compliance, on-call gaps, and unrealistic delivery commitments.

## When it breaks

Registers become archives, severity is inflated, acceptance is implicit, and escalation surprises managers. Review whether the owner has authority, whether evidence changed, and whether mitigation actually reduces consequence.

## Practice

**Build:** create a risk register for an AI platform launch, then write one escalation memo with options and residual risk owner. **Break:** an executive asks you to remove the risk from the launch record. **Explain back:** preserve professional duty while keeping the escalation factual.

## Check yourself

1. Who may accept residual risk?
2. Which conditions justify escalation beyond the usual chain?
3. Why include the consequence of no decision?
