# Ethics, power, and professional duty

Senior technical judgment includes responsibility for who bears harm, who can contest a system, and when professional duty must outrank convenience.

## Why it matters

Technical choices distribute access, surveillance, labor, cost, and error; calling them neutral hides power without removing it.

## How it works

Identify affected parties, including people absent from purchasing and design. Examine benefit, foreseeable harm, disparate impact, accessibility, privacy, labor displacement, environmental cost, and avenues for appeal. Distinguish legal permission, policy compliance, and ethical justification; they overlap but are not identical.

Use data minimization, proportionality, purpose limitation, meaningful consent, human review, and contestability where appropriate. Document evidence limits and who may accept residual risk. Invite domain expertise and affected perspectives without treating participation as ceremonial approval.

Power changes obligations. A staff engineer’s recommendation may silence less senior dissent even without formal authority. Credit contributors, disclose conflicts, avoid deceptive metrics, and preserve records of consequential concerns. Escalate credible risk through appropriate channels; for grave public harm, professional codes may require action beyond ordinary loyalty.

## See it yourself

An automated support triage reduces cost but deprioritizes messages written in one dialect. Aggregate resolution looks healthy. Slice evaluation, an appeal path, monitored human override, and launch limits turn a hidden distributional choice into governable evidence.

## Where it shows up

Ethical judgment applies to model evaluation, employee monitoring, abuse controls, accessibility, training data, experimentation, incident disclosure, and deadline pressure.

## When it breaks

Ethics becomes a checklist, affected groups are consulted after commitment, fairness is reduced to one metric, or leaders suppress inconvenient evidence. Pause irreversible expansion, preserve findings, identify authority, and seek independent expertise.

## Practice

**Build:** write an impact assessment for AI support triage with affected groups, benefits, harms, slices, consent, appeal, monitoring, owner, and stop conditions. **Break:** leadership requests an aggregate-only dashboard before launch. **Explain back:** state your duty, escalation, and evidence requirements.

## Check yourself

1. Why is compliance not complete ethical justification?
2. How can informal technical authority suppress dissent?
3. What makes an appeal mechanism meaningful?
