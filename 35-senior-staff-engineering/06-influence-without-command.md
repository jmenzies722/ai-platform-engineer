# Influence without command

Influence is the earned ability to improve a shared decision by making evidence, consequences, and commitments easier for others to act on.

## Why it matters

Cross-team work fails when advocates confuse visibility with trust, agreement with commitment, or organizational rank with informed consent.

## How it works

Map who decides, supplies expertise, executes, bears consequences, can block through policy, and is missing from the room. Listen for incentives and prior harm before proposing. Adapt detail to the audience without changing facts. A product leader needs user consequence; an operator needs failure and recovery; an executive needs the choice and strategic exposure.

Build credibility through accurate claims, explicit uncertainty, follow-through, and reciprocal help. Use prototypes and pilots when behavior is more persuasive than rhetoric. Ask for concrete commitments with owner, action, date, and evidence. Treat objections as information until evidence shows otherwise.

Influence has ethical limits. Do not manufacture urgency, hide burden, recruit private support to silence affected teams, or use mentoring relationships as political leverage. Escalate transparently when authority or values, rather than understanding, block a consequential decision.

## See it yourself

A polished platform presentation produces applause but no migration. A stakeholder map reveals that service owners bear on-call cost and lack staffing. A funded pilot, migration tooling, and explicit support commitment change the mechanism; another persuasive deck would not.

## Where it shows up

Influence drives standards, deprecations, reliability programs, platform adoption, and incident remediation where reporting lines do not match system boundaries.

## When it breaks

Common failures are consensus as veto, hidden coalition building, excessive meetings, and an advocate who becomes the only interpreter. Inspect unresolved claims, incentives, burden, trust, and decision rights before changing communication style.

## Practice

**Build:** create a stakeholder, burden, and decision map for a six-team platform migration. Prepare operator, product, and executive briefs with identical facts. **Break:** remove one team’s migration capacity and surface prior platform distrust. **Explain back:** change the mechanism, not merely the message.

## Check yourself

1. What turns alignment into commitment?
2. When does private pre-alignment become exclusion?
3. Which conflict requires authority rather than persuasion?
