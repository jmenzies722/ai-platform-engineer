# AI platform judgment

AI platform leadership decides which capabilities should become shared constraints, which should remain experiments, and how uncertain quality can be governed without freezing learning.

## Why it matters

Centralizing too early creates an expensive bottleneck; centralizing too late leaves identity, evaluation, cost, safety, and incident response inconsistent across products.

## How it works

Start with repeated user problems and organizational risk, not a platform mandate. Inventory application journeys, provider use, data classes, evaluation maturity, incidents, spend, and team capability. Centralize controls whose inconsistency creates systemic harm or waste: identity, secret handling, audit, quotas, approved connectivity, lineage, and evaluation contracts.

Keep domain behavior near product teams: prompts, task measures, retrieval policy, and user experience often need local ownership within shared interfaces. Offer a paved path with escape hatches, explicit residual responsibility, and a contribution model. Evaluate build, buy, and thin-integration options across portability, latency, compliance, operations, lock-in, and exit.

Govern artifact versions, rollout, rollback, provider changes, evaluation slices, and incident responsibility. Quality gates must reflect task consequence, not one universal score. Human review and policy services retain authority for consequential effects. Measure adoption, duplicated effort removed, incident reduction, developer lead time, quality, and cost per useful outcome.

## See it yourself

Teams ask for a universal agent runtime. Evidence shows their common needs are workload identity, tool authorization, traces, budgets, and evaluation records, while orchestration patterns change monthly. Standardizing contracts and controls preserves learning better than owning every loop.

## Where it shows up

Judgment is needed for gateways, model catalogs, retrieval services, evaluation platforms, prompt registries, agent runtimes, feature stores, and GPU capacity.

## When it breaks

Platforms fail through mandatory adoption without value, generic quality gates, opaque provider abstraction, central prompt ownership, and no migration funding. Revisit the user problem, systemic risk, and thinnest useful contract.

## Practice

**Build:** propose an AI platform portfolio with centralized controls, local responsibilities, paved path, escape process, build-buy analysis, governance, measures, and sunset criteria. **Break:** a model provider changes behavior and one regulated product cannot use the standard path. **Explain back:** preserve safety and local accountability without forking every control.

## Check yourself

1. Which AI concerns benefit most from centralization?
2. Why can a universal abstraction reduce portability?
3. What evidence shows a platform creates value rather than adoption theater?
