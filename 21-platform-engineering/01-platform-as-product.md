# Platforms as products

An internal platform succeeds when it solves repeated user problems better than teams can solve them separately.

## Why it matters

A tool mandate can centralize frustration without reducing cognitive load. Product thinking starts with users, journeys, constraints, and measurable outcomes.

## How it works

Identify user segments and interview them around recent work, not desired features. Map journeys such as creating, changing, operating, and retiring a service. Find repeated friction that a shared capability can address.

Define a platform mission, boundaries, service levels, ownership, roadmap, and support model. Start with a thin capability that proves value. Treat adoption as feedback; voluntary use is strong evidence when alternatives remain visible.

## See it yourself

"Teams need Kubernetes access" is a solution statement. "Teams wait four days for a safe, observable service environment" identifies a problem and measurable delay.

## Where it shows up

Internal cloud foundations, delivery systems, data platforms, ML platforms, and security services all have internal customers and lifecycle obligations.

## When it breaks

Leadership funds technology before discovery, the platform optimizes for its operators, every exception becomes a feature, or support load is omitted from capacity.

## Practice

Interview one team about its last deployment. Record evidence, frequency, consequence, current workaround, and a falsifiable outcome metric.

## Check yourself

1. What evidence distinguishes a problem from a requested solution?
2. Why is adoption insufficient without outcome data?
