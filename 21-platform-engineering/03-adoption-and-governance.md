# Adoption, governance, and measurement

Platform governance should make safe behavior easy and risk decisions explicit while measurement proves whether the platform improves delivery.

## Why it matters

Portal visits and resource counts measure activity, not value. Heavy approval gates drive work outside the platform and hide actual risk.

## How it works

Measure a balanced set: task success, lead time, failure and recovery, developer effort, support demand, platform reliability, security posture, and unit cost. Segment results; an average can hide one blocked user group.

Encode stable rules as policy with immediate explanations and remediation. Reserve human review for contextual risk. Publish deprecation windows, compatibility, migration help, and decision records. Maintain a feedback loop from support and exceptions into roadmap discovery.

## See it yourself

If adoption rises from 30% to 80% while deployment lead time and support tickets worsen, interface consolidation did not produce the intended outcome.

## Where it shows up

Scorecards, policy engines, quota systems, platform SLOs, cost allocation, office hours, and deprecation programs.

## When it breaks

Metrics become team rankings, mandatory adoption masks dissatisfaction, policy errors lack remediation, or old paths are removed before equivalent capability exists.

## Practice

Define five measures for one platform journey. For each, state data source, owner, segmentation, target behavior, and gaming risk.

## Check yourself

1. Which measures demonstrate reduced cognitive load?
2. When should policy require human judgment?
