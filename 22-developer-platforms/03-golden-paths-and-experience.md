# Golden paths and developer experience

A golden path integrates documentation, interfaces, defaults, and feedback so a common task is understandable from intent through operation.

## Why it matters

A portal can aggregate links while leaving the hard work unchanged. Developer experience improves when the platform removes decisions that do not differentiate the product and makes remaining decisions legible.

## How it works

Design from a real journey. Offer progressive disclosure: safe defaults first, advanced controls when needed, underlying evidence always reachable. Keep CLI, API, Git, and portal interfaces backed by the same capability contracts.

Documentation belongs beside action and should explain prerequisites, guarantees, limits, failure recovery, and ownership. Measure task completion, time, errors, support demand, and perceived effort with both telemetry and interviews.

## See it yourself

Count steps to diagnose a failed deployment, including context switches and waiting. A one-click start with opaque failure may be worse than a five-step flow with precise status.

## Where it shows up

Getting-started paths, deployment pages, service health views, documentation search, and contextual remediation.

## When it breaks

The portal becomes a mandatory bottleneck, abstractions hide incident evidence, documentation describes obsolete versions, or satisfaction scores replace task outcomes.

## Practice

Usability-test one platform journey with a developer. Observe silently; record hesitation, errors, recovery, and questions before proposing features.

## Check yourself

1. What distinguishes a golden path from a template?
2. Why must abstractions expose underlying evidence?
